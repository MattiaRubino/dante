# 1. Definition

A **Possibility** is the persistent scoped representation of a materially relevant candidate future that has not yet been adopted as an intended outcome or execution commitment and that LifeOS may preserve, explore, evaluate, compare, revisit, dismiss or mature without asserting that the candidate is a Goal, Proposal, Decision, Plan, Activity, preference or established fact.

It answers:

> **Which candidate future is being kept open for possible consideration, without claiming that it has already been adopted, decided, planned or made actionable?**

Classification:

```text
Possibility
= SCOPED PERSISTENT CANDIDATE-FUTURE / PRE-COMMITMENT SEMANTIC

NEW SCOPED SEMANTIC OWNER
YES

UNIVERSAL HYPOTHETICAL / UNCERTAINTY ROOT
NO
```

Possibility exists only where retaining the candidate as one continuing semantic item materially helps LifeOS preserve context, exploration, comparison, provenance or later transition history. A fleeting thought or generated suggestion does not automatically require persistent Possibility identity.

---

# 2. Current-LifeOS need

The accepted LifeOS North Star requires LifeOS to work with possibilities and undeveloped ideas before they become precise Goals, to discover possibilities without silently converting them into action or Decisions, and to allow ideas to remain inactive or mature over time.

Without Possibility semantics, the current kernel must choose among materially false substitutions:

```text
Goal
→ falsely asserts intentional adoption as desired

Proposal
→ falsely asserts a materially specific proposing act/context

Decision
→ falsely asserts resolution of a bounded question

Plan / Activity
→ falsely asserts execution structure/actionability

Content Artifact / Note
→ preserves wording/content but not the candidate-future posture
```

The required distinction is therefore not `idea` as a UI noun. It is the durable difference between:

```text
candidate future still open
!= intentionally adopted desired future
!= proposed candidate to another context
!= resolved choice
!= execution structure
!= information-bearing content
```

---

# 3. Core boundary — Possibility versus Goal

Goal is intentionally adopted desired-state semantics. Possibility is pre-adoption candidate-future semantics.

```text
Possibility
"One day I might like to spend a month in Japan."

Goal
"I am going to organize a month in Japan next spring."
```

Natural language such as `I would like`, `maybe`, `one day`, `could`, or `what if` does not mechanically determine classification. The semantic question is whether the candidate has actually been adopted as something intentionally desired/pursued in the applicable LifeOS context.

Canonical rule:

```text
Possibility != Goal

considering / liking / exploring
!= adoption as a Goal
```

A Possibility may later lead to a Goal. That transition does not turn the historical Possibility into a Goal state value and must not rewrite the earlier pre-commitment period as though adoption already existed.

---

# 4. Possibility versus Proposal

Proposal is a contextual semantic act through which an Actor puts a materially specific candidate forward for consideration. Possibility does not require a proposing act, recipient or proposal context.

```text
Possibility P
"Maybe move the holiday to September."

Proposal PR
Actor A formally puts "move the holiday to September" forward
for Actor B / shared-group consideration.
```

Therefore:

```text
Possibility != Proposal
```

A Proposal may be derived from, refer to or put forward a Possibility. The same candidate content does not merge the identities/semantics.

---

# 5. Possibility versus Decision

Decision is bounded resolution semantics. Possibility is deliberately compatible with non-resolution.

```text
Possibility
candidate remains open

Decision
bounded question materially resolved
```

Dismissing a Possibility does not automatically create Decision semantics. A Decision exists only where an actual bounded question was resolved with Decision-level meaning/history.

Likewise:

```text
Possibility dismissed
!= permanent negative preference
!= proof that the candidate will never be reconsidered
```

---

# 6. Possibility versus Plan / Activity / Event

```text
Possibility != Plan
Possibility != Activity
Possibility != Event
```

A Possibility may exist with no execution structure and no schedule.

A Plan may later coordinate how an adopted direction is pursued. An Activity is directly executable intended action. Event is occurrence-centred semantics whose temporal placement is intrinsic to its meaning.

An external Event may **surface** a Possibility without becoming that Possibility:

```text
meteor shower Event
+ known astrophotography interest
→ LifeOS surfaces "possible photography trip"

Event != Possibility
```

The candidate may disappear or remain open even though the source Event continues to exist independently.

---

# 7. Possibility versus Content Artifact / Idea

Content Artifact owns reusable information-content identity. Possibility owns candidate-future posture.

```text
Content Artifact
note: "Maybe spend a month in Japan"

Possibility
the retained candidate future "spend a month in Japan"
```

The same Artifact may describe several Possibilities; one Possibility may be supported by several Artifacts. Neither cardinality manufactures identity equality.

`Idea` remains bounded product/natural-language vocabulary rather than a separate universal kernel root.

Depending on use:

```text
idea as retained information/content
→ Content Artifact may suffice

idea as a candidate future kept open for consideration
→ Possibility semantics

idea already intentionally adopted as desired outcome
→ Goal semantics may apply
```

This avoids creating an ontology root merely because `idea` is a common product noun.

---

# 8. Someday / Maybe / Aspiration / Opportunity language

`Someday` / `Maybe` are product, query, grouping or lifecycle vocabulary. They are not separate native roots.

A future `Someday` surface may include, depending on product behavior, a Possibility and/or another already-owned concept that is intentionally inactive. The UI grouping does not change semantic identity.

`Aspiration` is likewise not accepted as a separate universal root. A vague aspiration may be represented as a Possibility while it remains pre-adoption; when intentionally adopted as a desired state it crosses into Goal semantics.

`Opportunity` is bounded product/specialist vocabulary, not a universal LifeOS root. In a CRM it may have domain-specific identity/lifecycle as a sales deal in progress. In LifeOS UI it may label a surfaced Possibility when useful, but market/provider vocabulary does not determine kernel ontology.

---

# 9. Origin, discovery and provenance

Possibility identity is independent from how the candidate was discovered.

Possible origins include:

```text
user thought/capture
conversation
Content Artifact
external Event
Observation
another Person
provider/import
AI/system discovery
```

Source/provenance does not itself establish user desire, preference, truth or adoption.

```text
system-discovered Possibility
!= user-owned preference
!= user intent
!= Goal
!= Decision
```

A transient AI suggestion is not automatically persisted as a Possibility. Persistent identity is warranted only when authorized product behavior retains the candidate and its continued review/exploration/history materially matters.

Where the source or derivation is consequential, accepted Provenance/Reconciliation semantics must remain reconstructible.

---

# 10. Evaluation and feasibility

LifeOS may explore a Possibility through feasibility, cost, timing, constraints, alternatives, resources, risk or likely consequences without treating that work as commitment.

```text
feasible
!= adopted

highly ranked
!= Goal

recommended by AI
!= Decision

resources available
!= Resource Allocation

calendar space exists
!= Schedule reservation
```

Criterion/Evaluation semantics may be used where an explicit evaluative basis is materially useful. Evaluation results do not mutate the Possibility into another semantic family automatically.

---

# 11. Identity, material change and history

Possibility identity may persist across ordinary refinement when the same candidate future remains under consideration.

Examples that may preserve identity:

```text
more detail added
cost estimate refined
source corrected
candidate wording improved
feasibility evidence added
review date changed
```

A materially different candidate future must not be treated as the same Possibility merely because the title or source is similar.

```text
same title != same Possibility automatically
same source != same Possibility automatically
same Artifact != same Possibility automatically
```

Accepted Version/material-state and Reconciliation semantics own material revision/correction mechanics where needed. No universal `status` state machine is established by this concept.

Historical transitions must remain reconstructible where material:

```text
T0 Possibility retained
T1 explored
T2 intentionally adopted as Goal G1
T3 Plan created
T4 Goal later abandoned
T5 similar Possibility reconsidered
```

The T0–T1 period must not be rewritten as historical Goal pursuit. Likewise a later abandoned Goal remains historically truthful and is not rewritten into `it was only ever a possibility`.

---

# 12. Multi-actor semantics

A Possibility may be personal or may become shared where product semantics justify one shared candidate.

Canonical rules:

```text
shared Possibility
!= shared endorsement
!= shared Goal
!= Agreement
!= Decision
!= Responsibility
!= Authority

creator/source actor
!= adopter
!= evaluator
!= decision-maker
```

Different Actors may hold different stances toward the same shared candidate without semantic duplication.

Visibility remains independent from source visibility:

```text
Visibility(Possibility)
!= Visibility(all private source context)
```

A system may use private authorized context internally to surface a candidate while exposing only a bounded consequence/recommendation. AI/system knowledge does not create disclosure permission.

External/accountless Persons may be sources, subjects or affected parties without Account identity becoming Possibility identity.

---

# 13. Resource, capacity and scheduling boundaries

Possibility existence does not reserve real-world capacity.

```text
Possibility exists
!= Resource Requirement automatically
!= Resource Allocation
!= Capacity Claim
!= Schedule
!= commitment
```

LifeOS may estimate what would be required if the candidate were adopted. Those estimates remain exploratory until the applicable Goal/Plan/Resource/Schedule semantics actually become effective.

This prevents large `Someday`/idea collections from manufacturing false resource demand or calendar pressure.

---

# 14. Scale and retention

LifeOS may surface many transient candidates. The kernel must not require one persistent Possibility for every generated suggestion, search result, advertisement, external event or hypothetical branch.

Persistent Possibility identity is appropriate only when retention as a continuing candidate materially matters.

The concept must remain compatible with:

- long-lived candidate lists;
- periodic review;
- dismissal without destructive historical rewriting where consequence matters;
- later reconsideration;
- many AI-generated transient alternatives without persistence explosion;
- offline/sync/provider imports without provider ID becoming canonical identity.

---

# 15. Anti-overmodeling / specialist boundary

Possibility is **not** a universal root for all uncertainty or future-looking information.

It must not absorb:

```text
uncertain factual claims
scientific hypotheses as a universal class
forecast/probability models
Risk ontology
Decision options universally
CRM sales Opportunity semantics
legal options/rights
medical differential diagnosis
financial trading opportunities
provider recommendation objects
```

Those realities remain with their owning concepts/specialist systems unless separate current LifeOS evidence demonstrates a general-kernel need.

Rejected expansions include:

```text
Idea root
Someday root
Maybe root
Aspiration root
Opportunity universal root
Hypothesis / PossibleThing universal superclass
single status enum that turns Possibility into Goal/Plan/Decision
```
