# LifeOS V1 — Health, Nutrition, and Wellness Boundaries

## Principle

LifeOS V1 is a personal organisation, lifestyle, and wellbeing product. It may help users organise meals, training, recovery, rehabilitation instructions, and other health-adjacent activities, but it is not presented as a doctor, dietitian, nutritionist, therapist, or other regulated professional.

A disclaimer is necessary, but it is not sufficient by itself. Product behaviour, marketing, prompts, generated content, and user flows must remain consistent with the declared non-medical purpose.

## User-facing disclosure

Before generating or materially changing a health-, nutrition-, rehabilitation-, or safety-relevant plan, LifeOS should clearly explain that:

- the output is generated for organisation and general wellbeing purposes;
- it may be incomplete or wrong;
- it is not a diagnosis, prescription, treatment, or substitute for a qualified professional;
- known allergies, medical conditions, medications, pregnancy, eating disorders, rehabilitation restrictions, or other relevant constraints must be disclosed by the user;
- urgent symptoms or emergencies must not be handled through LifeOS.

The disclosure should appear contextually when it matters, not only inside long terms and conditions.

## Product behaviour must match the disclosure

LifeOS must not:

- claim to diagnose, treat, cure, prevent, predict, or clinically monitor a disease;
- prescribe or change medication;
- present generated content as a professional prescription;
- override an imported professional plan silently;
- imply that a warning label eliminates the risks created by medical functionality;
- use marketing language that contradicts the product's stated wellness purpose.

## Nutrition boundary

LifeOS may support:

- meal organisation and scheduling;
- recipe and grocery planning;
- general wellness-oriented meal suggestions;
- user-defined calorie, macronutrient, ingredient, budget, preference, allergy, and dietary constraints;
- import and scheduling of a plan created by a qualified professional;
- detection of obvious conflicts with known user constraints;
- tracking of user-reported intake and progress.

The V1 should not present AI-generated content as a therapeutic or professionally prescribed diet.

Requests involving disease treatment, clinically significant malnutrition, eating disorders, pregnancy complications, medication interactions, severe allergies, paediatric needs, or other high-risk conditions should be limited, referred to a qualified professional, or handled only by organising instructions already supplied by that professional.

Whether highly personalised diet generation is offered publicly must receive specific Italian legal review because diet formulation is associated with regulated professional activities.

## Imported professional plans

When a user imports a plan from a doctor, dietitian, nutritionist, physiotherapist, or other qualified professional, LifeOS primarily:

- extracts and schedules the instructions;
- preserves the source and original document;
- distinguishes professional instructions from AI suggestions;
- warns before any change that may alter the professional plan;
- allows the user to keep an exception temporary;
- does not claim that its transformation has been approved by the professional.

## Context and safety checks

Before suggesting a plan, LifeOS checks relevant active constraints, including allergies, coeliac disease, injuries, rehabilitation limits, medications when supplied, accessibility needs, existing programs, and professional instructions.

A known incompatibility should block or clearly flag the proposal. Missing information should not be silently invented.

## Risk-based interaction

Low-risk organisational actions may proceed normally. Higher-risk requests require progressively stronger controls, such as:

- a contextual warning;
- explicit confirmation of known constraints;
- a narrower general-wellness answer;
- use of an imported professional plan instead of generating a new one;
- recommendation to consult a qualified professional;
- refusal to generate a plan when a safe non-medical response is not possible.

## Presentation and marketing

The same boundary must be respected in:

- onboarding;
- terms and privacy information;
- plan previews;
- assistant responses;
- app-store descriptions;
- website and advertising copy;
- help documentation;
- notifications and exports.

LifeOS must not use medical claims in promotional materials while relying on a wellness disclaimer elsewhere.

## Example disclosure

> LifeOS provides organisational and general wellbeing support. It is not a doctor, dietitian, nutritionist, therapist, or medical device, and its suggestions may contain errors. Do not use it for diagnosis, treatment, medication changes, or emergencies. Review health- and nutrition-related plans carefully and consult a qualified professional when appropriate.
