# LifeOS V1 — Scheduling Flexibility and Replanning

## Principle

Scheduling flexibility is not represented by one simple fixed/flexible flag. Each planning item can combine several independent rules so LifeOS can understand what may move, what must stay inside a boundary, what may be split, and what should happen when the original plan is no longer realistic.

## Time constraint

An item may use one of these temporal constraints:

- fixed instant or fixed block: it must remain at the scheduled date and time unless the user explicitly overrides it;
- bounded window: it may move only inside a defined date or time range;
- deadline-constrained: it may be scheduled freely before a latest completion date;
- preferred window: it should stay inside a preferred range, but may leave it when the user approves a better plan;
- open scheduling: it may be placed wherever compatible capacity exists.

An item inside a hard date range must not be moved outside that range automatically.

## Movement policy

Movement can be:

- locked;
- movable inside its valid window;
- movable only after confirmation;
- freely replannable according to priorities and constraints.

The policy can differ between a single occurrence and the originating routine or program.

## Session structure

An item can be:

- indivisible;
- divisible into shorter sessions;
- partially completable;
- allowed to finish early when the required result is reached;
- allowed to merge with compatible sessions;
- required to preserve minimum session duration, recovery, preparation, or spacing rules.

A program may therefore complete earlier than expected when its measurable outcome is reached, while still preserving quality and safety constraints.

## Persistence and recurrence

An item may be:

- one-off and disposable after execution, cancellation, or expiry;
- a single occurrence of a recurring series;
- generated by a routine;
- part of a multi-step program;
- a reusable template that creates future items.

Changes can apply to:

- this occurrence only;
- this occurrence and selected linked items;
- all future occurrences;
- the complete routine or program.

## Failure and fallback policy

When an item can no longer be completed as planned, its configured or inferred fallback may be:

- skip without replacement;
- postpone inside the same valid range;
- move to another valid date;
- replace with an equivalent alternative;
- shorten or split into multiple sessions;
- preserve only the highest-priority portion;
- replan dependent items;
- replan the surrounding day, week, or whole remaining program.

LifeOS should not blindly move one item when doing so creates a worse plan. It can detect that the original structure is no longer feasible and propose a broader replan.

## Replanning scope

Replanning can operate at different scopes:

- selected item;
- surrounding blocks and dependencies;
- current day;
- current week;
- remaining date range;
- remaining routine or program.

The system should choose the smallest scope that produces a valid and useful result, then expand the scope only when necessary.

## User control

Hard constraints are never violated silently. Material changes show:

- what will move or be replaced;
- which limits remain protected;
- which goals, deadlines, or dependencies are affected;
- whether the change applies once or changes the future plan.

The user can confirm, edit, reject, or undo a proposal. Common unambiguous low-risk operations may be applied directly only when enabled by the user.

## Examples

### Bounded program

A study program must be completed between 1 September and 30 November. Sessions may move and split inside that range, but LifeOS must not schedule them in December unless the user changes the program boundary.

### Early completion

A course contains twenty planned sessions, but the user reaches the required measurable level after sixteen. LifeOS may propose closing the program early or converting the remaining sessions into optional maintenance work.

### One-off item

A temporary errand is relevant only today. If it expires, the user may discard it rather than carrying it forward indefinitely.

### Broader replan

Several sessions have been missed and the remaining plan no longer fits the available weeks. LifeOS should propose a new structure, such as fewer longer sessions, reduced scope, a changed deadline, or a different method, rather than repeatedly pushing every missed item forward.
